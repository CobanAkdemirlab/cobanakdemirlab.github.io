Computational Genomics · UTHealth Houston School of Public Health

From computational analysis to disease mechanisms.

We are a computational genomics lab working out how genetic variants are associated with disease and building the tools to find them. Our work spans nonsense-mediated mRNA decay, rare disease gene discovery, congenital heart disease, and multimodal AI that links clinical data with genomes.

NMD-escape

Last exon: no downstream exon junction to flag the transcript

The mRNA survives and a truncated protein is made. Depending on what the missing tail did, it can act as a dominant-negative or gain-of-function allele. Finding the genes where these escape alleles are associated with disease is the core of our NMD work.

01 · Research

Research

We combine population-scale sequencing, allele-specific expression, machine learning, and deep phenotyping to understand how variants are associated with disease. Our aim is to find the genes, variant types, and inheritance models that standard single-variant, single-gene pipelines miss, and to turn them into tools for diagnosis.

Flagship

NMD-escape disease genes

We predict whether a protein-truncating variant triggers or escapes nonsense-mediated decay, combining the canonical rules with non-canonical features learned from allele-specific expression in population cohorts. We then look for genes where escaping alleles are associated with disease, potentially through dominant-negative or gain-of-function effects.

  • Machine-learning models of NMD outcome prediction trained on large-scale allele-specific expression datasets
  • Genome-scale stop codon scanning in collaboration with the Jagannathan lab
  • Phase separation propensity and intrinsically disordered regions in NMD-escape regions of disease genes
  • NMD-escape protein-truncating variants in autism (SSC, SPARK) and quantitative blood traits (TOPMed)
Cardiovascular · AI

Congenital heart disease & multimodal AI

CHD is the most common birth defect, yet most cases remain genetically unexplained. We study inheritance beyond the monogenic model, from NODAL variants spanning simple transposition to heterotaxy, to digenic combinations in laterality defects.

  • Digenic and oligogenic inheritance models for laterality defects and left-sided lesions
  • Multimodal AI models that predict likely genetic diagnoses from echocardiograms, imaging reports, and electronic health records (NIH Common Fund)
  • Variant prioritization that integrates these predictions to rank coding and non-coding variants from exome and genome sequencing
Rare disease

Rare disease gene discovery

As investigators in the NHGRI GREGoR Consortium since 2021, we develop methods for the variants and inheritance models that single-gene analysis cannot explain, and apply them to families across the rare-disease spectrum.

  • Cell-type-resolved computational frameworks for digenic and oligogenic variant discovery
  • The Turkish population variome: how runs of homozygosity and recent haplotypes shape rare disease traits in 773 affected individuals and 643 unaffected relatives
  • Multilocus pathogenic variation contributing to clinical heterogeneity among affected siblings
  • Chromosomal inversions and intragenic duplications as underrecognized variant classes associated with Mendelian disease
  • Graph-genome approaches for variant detection in complex, medically relevant regions, in collaboration with Fritz Sedlazeck
Population genomics

Transcriptomes in blood and beyond

Cohorts with matched genomes and RNA let us measure how variants change expression in people rather than only predict it. We use them to calibrate our models and to find RNA signatures of disease risk.

  • Allele-specific expression analysis of matched genome and RNA-seq data from TOPMed and GTEx
  • Longitudinal transcriptomic profiling of FVIII inhibitor development in severe hemophilia A
  • Rare variant association studies of sudden cardiac arrest and von Willebrand factor levels

NMD Escape Intolerance

Ranks genes by how strongly truncating variants in their NMD-escape region are depleted in the population, flagging candidates for dominant disease.

04 · Team

People

Principal Investigator

Zeynep H. Coban-Akdemir, PhD

Also published as Zeynep Coban Akdemir and Zeynep C. Akdemir

Assistant Professor of Epidemiology, Human Genetics & Environmental Sciences · Adjunct Faculty, Baylor College of Medicine Human Genome Sequencing Center

Zeynep is a computational geneticist who studies how protein-truncating variants are associated with disease and builds methods, including multimodal AI, to diagnose rare and congenital disorders. She trained in human genetics with James R. Lupski at Baylor College of Medicine, where she developed the NMD-escape intolerance score and methods to find genes whose truncated transcripts are associated with dominant disease traits. Since 2021 she has been an investigator in the NHGRI GREGoR Consortium. She teaches Genetic Epidemiology and Epidemiology I and has served on thirteen graduate thesis committees.

07 · Opportunities

Join the lab

We welcome people from genetics, epidemiology, statistics, and computer science. Comfort with programming matters more than a particular background. Trainees work inside GREGoR, TOPMed, and SFARI collaborations and present at national meetings such as ASHG.

Ph.D. students

Apply through the UTHealth Houston School of Public Health doctoral programs in Epidemiology or Biostatistics and mention the lab in your statement. Rotations and practicum projects are available.

Postdocs

We look for candidates with experience in human genetics, variant interpretation, or machine learning on genomic data. Send a CV, a short research statement, and contacts for two references.

Visiting students

Undergraduate and visiting graduate students can join for summer or semester-long computational projects with mentoring from the team.
